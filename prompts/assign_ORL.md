## ORL — Organizational Readiness Level

Core question: **Does it fit users' existing work practices?**

| Bin | Levels | Phase | Meaning | Key question |
|-----|--------|-------|---------|--------------|
| Low | 1–3 | Mismatch | Fundamental break with existing practices; integration unclear or only vaguely imagined | Does it break current workflows? |
| Mid | 4–6 | Adaptation | Integration described/planned; major-to-moderate org changes needed | Can processes adapt to fit it? |
| High | 7–9 | Integration | Minor changes only → works seamlessly with existing processes | Does it fit seamlessly? |

Strong evidence types: adoption case studies, workflow-integration accounts, training/support requirements, user testimonials.
Typical sources: trade press, case studies, extension-service reports, user forums.

Note: ORL evidence is often scarce. Look for accounts of how people actually use the technology in their daily work, not just that they use it.

Physical delivery-format compatibility (e.g., the product is a seed, powder, liquid, or spray that existing equipment can handle) is necessary but NOT sufficient for High. You must also confirm from the evidence that adoption does not require significant new training, infrastructure, supply chains, or process redesign beyond receiving and applying the product. A technology still in R&D or lab-to-field transition should not be assessed as High based on its planned or eventual delivery format.

For technologies where evidence confirms both delivery-format compatibility AND demonstrated real-world adoption with only minor workflow changes (e.g., feed additives mixed into existing rations with no new training, seed treatments applied with standard equipment at commercial scale), the absence of reported integration challenges is itself evidence of seamless fit — this supports High, not an evidence gap.
